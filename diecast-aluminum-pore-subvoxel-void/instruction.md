I run porosity QA on high-pressure die-cast aluminum housings for EV inverters. Molten AlSi10Mg injected at 700C traps dissolved hydrogen that precipitates during solidification and leaves sealed internal gas pores. Those become leak paths and fatigue sites. We X-ray housings with a 225 kV cone-beam industrial CT rig.

The imaging itself lies. The tube focal spot is not a point, the flat-panel scintillator spreads light, and there is scatter inside the thick aluminum wall. Combined effect is a blur that keeps total photon count energy but pushes it outward into a wide fuzzy halo. On screen a pore that is actually compact looks like a huge glowing cloud. Customer wants the real geometric pore size in cubic millimetres, not the apparent cloud size.

You must write the tool that does this.

Save it as /app/solve.py. Grader runs python /app/solve.py with a scan path argument and takes the final whitespace token from stdout as your answer.

We include one housing scan to develop with at /app/data/scene.adpv. You can open that while coding. The hidden evaluation set uses completely different scans that vary in grid shape, voxel pitch, aluminum bulk density, pore brightness, scatter width, background level, and noise. They are passed as randomly named temporary files. Do not embed numbers or absolute paths from the example file. Any solution that hardcodes the example volume fails the hidden checks.

File format is .adpv, a small custom container, all little endian. The first four bytes are ASCII magic ADPV. Bytes four to seven hold uint32 version. Bytes eight to eleven hold uint32 data type tag where two means signed int16 and sixteen means float32. Bytes twelve to twenty three hold three uint32 dimensions nx ny nz which are voxels along x y z. Bytes twenty four to thirty five hold three float32 scale factors sx sy sz in millimetres per voxel. These are anisotropic and file dependent so you must read them. Bytes thirty six to thirty nine hold uint32 offset to start of voxel block. From that offset onward there are nx times ny times nz samples in the declared type, x moves fastest, so linear address for x y z is x plus nx times y plus ny times z. In the file dark voids were inverted during export so gas pores appear bright.

What is inside each file. A single dominant sealed hydrogen gas pore is the one we grade plus sometimes one or two tiny isolated bright dots far away caused by microporosity or lubricant inclusions. Those dots must not be counted. To get rid of them keep only the principal connected structure using twenty six neighbour volumetric connectivity and choose the component with the most integrated excess signal. The pore interior has a flat saturated plateau, edges are diluted by partial volume and then further smeared by the X-ray blur. The rest of the housing is a roughly flat aluminum matrix brightness plus detector noise.

Why naive voxel counting does not work here. If you count everything above a generous brightness you absorb the diffuse halo that surrounds every pore and the result is roughly double the true physical size, sometimes more. If you count only very bright voxels you miss the thin shrinkage fingers and dendrite channels that connect to the main bubble which never become fully bright because of partial filling and you end up at about half the true size. No single global level works across the product line because overall gain, wall thickness, blur width and noise floor all shift per batch. Counting bright voxels is a proxy, integrating total energy is the physical invariant.

Blur conserves counts. That is the key that allows sub voxel recovery. But using it correctly is a small pipeline of its own. First get an unbiased estimate of background aluminum level without letting the pore itself bias you, next get a robust estimate of background scatter which is noise sigma, then isolate the main pore energy from the far specks, then get the real interior signal level that would be seen without blur and without being tricked by detector noise spikes, then grow outward to capture the faint halo belonging to the pore until the halo average excess falls into the noise while stopping before you accidentally bridge to a distant micropore. Skipping halo capture causes systematic under estimation because a large fraction of energy lives in the halo when scatter is wide, including specks causes over estimation.

We grade to three percent relative error. Simple level methods are far outside that window.
